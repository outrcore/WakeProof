# Product

## One-sentence product

WakeProof is an iOS alarm that requires credible proof that the user got out of bed and then verifies that the user stayed awake.

## Problem

Ordinary alarms fail a high-pain segment of users because those users can:

- dismiss or snooze while barely conscious;
- solve familiar puzzles on autopilot;
- spoof weak photo matching;
- scan a nearby code and return to bed;
- disable or weaken tomorrow’s alarm before sleeping;
- complete one mission and immediately fall back asleep;
- lose trust after a snooze, recurrence, permission, or lifecycle failure.

The relevant customer is not “anyone who uses an alarm.” It is a person for whom waking up is repeatedly difficult or consequential.

## Core promise

> **Prove you’re awake.**

The product should deliver a three-stage wake protocol:

1. **Get out of bed** with photo, barcode, object, or movement proof.
2. **Turn the brain on** with a short cognitive challenge.
3. **Stay awake** with a delayed verification check and re-alert if missed.

## Jobs to be done

### Functional

- Wake me by my latest acceptable time.
- Force me to leave the bed.
- Prevent half-asleep me from taking an easy shortcut.
- Re-alert after snooze every time.
- Catch me if I return to sleep.
- Give me a safe escape when a legitimate situation makes the mission impossible.

### Emotional

- Let me trust tomorrow morning before I go to sleep.
- Remove the shame and anxiety of repeatedly oversleeping.
- Make success feel earned rather than accidental.
- Let me share the challenge without sharing private bedroom or bathroom imagery.

### Social

- Help a roommate, partner, parent, teammate, or creator hold me accountable.
- Let creators demonstrate the product through entertaining failed-cheat attempts.
- Let friends share wake protocols without exposing private health data.

## Primary users

| Segment | Pain | Representative use case |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy snoozers | Repeated unconscious dismissal | Ordinary work or school morning |
| Shift workers | High-consequence irregular mornings | Nursing, logistics, emergency work |
| Students | Irregular schedules and exam pressure | Early exam or class |
| Travelers | High-consequence one-off wake-ups | Flight, train, event |
| Athletes and creators | Identity tied to morning routine | Training or content challenge |
| ADHD/executive-function users | Intention-action gap | Medication, work, or appointment |

WakeProof is not a medical treatment and should not imply that difficulty waking is merely a willpower failure.

## Positioning

### Category

Proof-of-wakefulness alarm.

### Competitive frame

Not “the alarm with the most missions.”

### Differentiation

- stronger live photo proof;
- a reliable, explicit snooze lifecycle;
- a delayed stay-awake check;
- pre-alarm commitment protection;
- safety-oriented emergency fallback;
- visible reliability diagnostics;
- local-first privacy.

### Positioning statement

For people who routinely outsmart normal alarms, WakeProof is the alarm that verifies a real wake sequence and checks that the user stayed awake. Unlike puzzle-only alarms, WakeProof treats waking as a protocol rather than a single dismissal action.

## Initial default protocol: Maddie Mode

- One permitted snooze.
- Fixed physical target such as a bathroom sink or coffee maker.
- Live, multi-frame photo proof.
- One short rotating cognitive task.
- Five-minute delayed wake check.
- Alarm editing locked during the final hour.
- Emergency escape available, recorded, and intentionally inconvenient.

This is a product hypothesis, not a permanent product name.

## Product principles

- **Trust before breadth.**
- **Latest wake time is sacred.**
- **Anti-cheat must not become anti-user.**
- **Safety exits always exist.**
- **No monetization during distress.**
- **Private spaces stay private.**
- **One clear recommended protocol beats fifteen unexplained toggles.**
- **Every failure creates diagnosable evidence.**
- **Probabilistic features must have deterministic fallbacks.**

## MVP non-goals

- full sleep tracking;
- snore recording;
- sleep-apnea detection;
- medical or diagnostic claims;
- social feed;
- financial penalties;
- AI-generated voices;
- Android;
- dozens of mission types;
- cloud accounts as a requirement;
- “optimal wake time” based on Apple Watch;
- complex Apple Health dashboards.

## Product moat hypothesis

Feature names are easy to clone. Durable advantage must come from:

- wake-session reliability;
- photo-verification calibration;
- a growing adversarial test corpus;
- failure telemetry and reconciliation;
- user trust and reviews;
- creator-led distribution;
- wake-protocol outcome data;
- rapid iteration on honest-pass and cheat-rejection tradeoffs.

## Success definition

A user should be able to say:

> “I trust this to wake me, it cannot be fooled by my usual tricks, and it catches me when I try to go back to bed.”
