# Launch

## Launch thesis

WakeProof should launch as a narrow, high-trust product with a content-native anti-cheat story—not as a broad sleep platform.

## Core message

**Prove you’re awake.**

Supporting messages:

- “For people who can solve alarms while half asleep.”
- “A real snooze that actually rings again.”
- “Photo proof plus a follow-up check.”
- “It catches you going back to bed.”
- “Built around a latest wake time you can trust.”

Avoid:

- “Impossible to cheat”
- “Guaranteed to wake everyone”
- “Clinically optimal wake time”
- “Diagnoses sleep problems”
- shame-based messaging about laziness

## Launch audience order

1. Heavy snoozers
2. Students and early-shift workers
3. Fitness and productivity creators
4. Travelers with critical mornings
5. ADHD/executive-function communities, with careful non-medical language
6. Apple Watch/sleep-optimization users after Watch features exist

## UGC creative system

### Repeatable format

1. Hook: creator claims they can cheat any alarm.
2. WakeProof alarm fires.
3. Creator tries screenshot, substitute object, or nearby shortcut.
4. Attempt is rejected.
5. Creator completes the real target.
6. Creator tries to return to bed.
7. Delayed check re-alerts.
8. Privacy-safe result card ends the video.

### Hook library

- “I downloaded an alarm I supposedly can’t cheat.”
- “Day seven of my coffee maker forcing me out of bed.”
- “This alarm caught me going back to sleep.”
- “I can do math in my sleep, so I tried this instead.”
- “My roommate chose what I had to photograph at 5:30.”
- “I tested every shortcut I could think of.”
- “The snooze actually rang again.”

- No-Snooze Challenge

## Productized growth

- Shareable protocol links
- Wake cards without private images
- Referral Pro days after invited verified wake-ups
- Creator attribution codes
- “Cheat Test” onboarding challenge
- Optional rejected-cheat count
- App Clip or web preview research later

## Beta phases

### Private alpha

- 20–30 adversarial heavy snoozers
- daily founder contact
- structured morning survey
- explicit permission testing
- no broad paid traffic

### Closed beta

- 100–500 users
- segmented cohorts
- creator seeding
- support and incident runbook
- pricing experiments limited by sample size

### Public launch

Only after release gates in [release-checklist.md](release-checklist.md) pass.

## App Store assets

### Screenshot story

1. Prove you’re awake
2. Get out of bed with live photo proof
3. Choose physical and cognitive missions
4. Snooze that reliably re-alerts
5. Stay-awake check
6. Commitment lock
7. Reliability Center
8. Private by design

### Preview video

Show the full protocol in under 30 seconds. Never use footage that implies access to a user’s camera while they are not actively completing a mission.

### Keyword hypotheses

- loud alarm
- heavy sleeper
- wake up
- alarm challenge
- photo alarm
- barcode alarm
- math alarm
- no snooze
- wake check

Research keyword competitiveness and Apple policy before final metadata.

## Launch gates

- Alarm lifecycle matrix passes
- No S0/S1 bugs
- Reliability SLO met in beta
- False rejects within target
- Emergency escape validated
- Privacy policy matches implementation
- App Store health/camera disclosures complete
- Subscription downgrade tested
- Support response prepared
- Five working creative concepts
- Referral instrumentation verified
- StoreKit products approved
- Production dashboards ready

## Post-launch cadence

### Daily for first two weeks

- review alarm incidents;
- review false-reject reasons;
- monitor refunds and reviews;
- respond to support;
- compare versions and OS cohorts;
- disable experiments that hurt reliability.

### Weekly

- review retention and protocol funnel;
- interview failed activations;
- refresh creative;
- ship only low-risk fixes unless a reliability incident requires more.

### Monthly

- pricing and unit-economics review;
- competitor refresh;
- roadmap gate review;
- decide whether Watch discovery has earned investment.
